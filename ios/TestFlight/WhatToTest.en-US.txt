New and changed

- One camera can have several saved setups - its own access point, a router, a hotspot, a cable - each remembered separately instead of overwriting each other.
- Stream Preset and Quality Bias are remembered per setup, so what you pick for the camera's own network no longer follows you onto a cable. A camera-access-point setup starts on Fast: that link is the narrowest one.
- Settings > Link > Processing: Feed Upscaler (Off, Fast, Quality, AI) and Feed Noise Reduction. AI infers detail the camera never captured, so judge critical focus on Quality or Fast.
- Display settings are built around the three DISP modes, with separate video and photo layouts. DISP 1 and DISP 2 have an "Edit view": tap the eye on any element to show or hide it, and DISP 2 starts with picture, side rail, focus box and batteries.
- The focus dial works in video as well as photo - off by default, in the FOCUS popup. Live view gains a magnifier: switch it on in the view-assist tools, pick 2x, 3x or 4x, and a key punches in on the focus point.
- Connecting is two choices instead of five: Wireless (camera access point, phone hotspot, or a Router you both join) and Cable Link. Cameras on 10.x networks are found now. One device can share what it sees: turn on Share This Feed in Settings > Link and others list it under Nearby Broadcasts, each with its own scopes and LUTs. On iPad the picture can come from the camera's HDMI output through a USB capture device, while exposure, focus, record and media keep working over the camera link. Playback gains a clean-view button, high-frame-rate clips can be watched at their edit speed, and de-squeeze works in photo mode too.

Fixes

- A router connection no longer asks you to join the camera's own network, and saved setups no longer read as offline while you are on that network.
- A weak or busy wireless link degrades the picture instead of dropping the session.
- The occasional single bright frame in live view should be gone. Please report it if you still see one.
- Changes you make on the camera reach the app quickly again. The aperture ring, ISO or shutter used to take twenty seconds to show - or in video, never.
- Photos shot as HEIF appear in the media browser at all, and connecting a second camera no longer changes its settings.
- Pickers show what your camera supports, in the order it uses.
- Unplugging the cable or power-cycling the camera used to wedge the app. Both recover on their own, or offer Retry connection.
- Command view keeps the timecode running.

What to test

- Connect the same body two ways - its own access point, then a router or cable. Each should be its own saved setup, and the Quality Bias you set on one should not move the other.
- Watch a dim high-ISO shot with Feed Noise Reduction on and off, then step the Feed Upscaler through Fast, Quality and AI on the same framing.
- Turn on Share This Feed on a connected device, then open the app on a second one: it should appear under Nearby Broadcasts and play the same picture.
- Switch the magnifier on, set it to 3x, and punch in while pulling focus by hand. On iPad, plug in an HDMI capture device and try Cable Link > HDMI.
- Turn the aperture ring while watching IRIS, then pull the cable mid-shot: IRIS should track close to real time, and the picture should hold the last frame and recover.
