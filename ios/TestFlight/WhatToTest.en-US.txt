New and changed

- Share what you see. Turn on Share This Feed in Settings > Link and other devices list it under Nearby Broadcasts, each with its own scopes and LUTs, with no network needed. A watcher can ask for camera control; while a watcher holds it, record presses from Apple Watch or a Bluetooth shutter are refused.
- One camera, many setups. Its own access point, a cable, a hotspot, and each Wi-Fi network you use are saved separately, shown as tabs on the camera's row, and renameable so "Wi-Fi (2)" can become "Studio". Stream Preset and Quality Bias are remembered per setup.
- Rotation, both ways. Turn the body on its side and the picture follows (video and photo), and the iPhone itself now works in both landscape directions. Settings > Controls has Auto-Rotate Feed if you want the monitoring image to stay put.
- Pinch the live view to zoom anywhere in the frame (the old fixed 2x/3x/4x magnifier is retired), and tap-to-focus follows the zoom. The focus dial works in video as well as photo, off by default in the FOCUS popup.
- Settings > Link > Processing: Feed Upscaler (Off, Fast, Quality, AI) and Feed Noise Reduction. AI infers detail the camera never captured, so judge critical focus on Quality or Fast. Link Health now shows the measured rate the link is actually carrying.
- On the wrist: photo mode with a shutter and a shots-remaining readout, the crown magnifies the preview and a drag pans it. On iPad the picture can come from the camera's HDMI output through a USB capture device while exposure, focus, record and media keep working over the camera link.

Fixes

- Answering "Start recording?" or "Stop recording?" now does exactly that. A take started or stopped on the camera while the prompt was open is no longer reversed.
- A refused record, such as no card or a full card, puts the Record button back to what the camera reports.
- The FPS readout no longer jumps after a late or repeated frame, such as during a Wi-Fi hiccup.
- Frame.io no longer asks you to sign in again after a token refresh.
- Original Z 7, Z 6, Z 5, and Z 50 now reach live view over USB-C, and USB-C reconnects after the cable is knocked loose or a few seconds in the background, without force-quitting the app.
- Original Z 6, Z 5, and Z 7 no longer get pairing steps they do not support. A Z 6III is no longer treated as an original Z 6. Settings > Share Diagnostics keeps a connect-attempt trace on the phone until you send it.
- Share and Save to Photos work on clips from the camera, including proxies whose media is shorter than the header. They used to fail with "Invalid sample cursor".
- Support, Privacy, and Terms in Settings now open at opencapture.org/openzcine, the project's new home. Old links keep working.

What to test

- Turn on Record Confirmation, tap Record, then start or stop the take on the camera body before you answer. The camera should end up the way you answered, and Record should match it.
- Share This Feed and hand camera control to a watcher, then press record on Apple Watch or a Bluetooth shutter: it should be refused. Take control back and both work again.
- If you were signed in to Frame.io before this update, upload a clip without signing in again. Watch the FPS readout across a Wi-Fi hiccup: it should stay steady.
- Plug in over USB-C, connect, then knock the cable loose and plug it back in. It should come back without force-quitting. On an original Z 6, Z 5, Z 7, or Z 6III, connect over USB-C and the camera's own Wi-Fi.
- Play a clip from the camera, tap Share, then Share and Save to Photos. Both should finish, with a spinner while Photos writes, without disconnecting first.
